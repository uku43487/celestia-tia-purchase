## Three realistic ways to buy TIA

**A centralized exchange.** Register, verify identity, fund the account, place an order. This is the route with actual customer support, order books you can inspect, and fiat on-ramps that work with a bank card. It's also the route where you're handing custody to someone else until you withdraw.

**A self-custody wallet with a built-in swap.** Wallets like Keplr and Leap support TIA natively because Celestia is Cosmos-SDK based, and some wallets let you swap into TIA directly. You keep the keys the whole time. The tradeoff is that you need crypto to start with, and pricing comes from a swap route rather than an order book.

**A DEX.** No registration, no KYC, connect a wallet and swap. Gas fees apply, and liquidity on smaller pairs can be thin enough that slippage matters more than the headline price.

For anyone buying their first TIA with a bank card, the exchange route is the practical one. ## Beyond spot: perpetuals and leveraged tokens

Spot is the right default. But TIA has a derivatives market too, and it's substantially more liquid than the spot market most days — spot volume in the low single-digit millions versus futures volume in the tens of millions [2][10].

Gate lists TIA/USDT perpetual contracts and the TIA3L and TIA3S leveraged tokens [3][4]. Perpetual futures at VIP 0 run **0.02% maker and 0.05% taker**, and they settle in the base or quote asset [8]. Leveraged tokens behave differently from a leveraged position — they rebalance daily, so holding them through a choppy market bleeds value in a way a futures position doesn't. If you're opening your first leveraged TIA trade, the perpetual with a stop is easier to reason about than a 3x token.

## Getting TIA off the exchange

Leaving TIA on an exchange is a custody decision, and it's worth making deliberately rather than by default. Self-custody options for TIA are relatively friendly: it's a Cosmos-SDK chain, so **Keplr** and **Leap** both hold it natively, and hardware wallets like **Ledger** support it for cold storage [11]. A Ledger paired with Keplr is the combination most people settle on for anything they don't plan to trade this month.

Before you withdraw, be careful about the network. Send a small test amount first — the withdrawal fee and the confirm time depend on the destination chain, and a test transaction costs you a few cents instead of an entire balance. Gate's own guide recommends exactly this, and it's the step people skip right before they open a support ticket [5].

**How much TIA can I buy with $100?**
At a mid-$0.30s price, roughly 270–290 TIA before fees. Prices move constantly, so treat that as a ballpark, not a quote. Gate's own converter page shows similar estimates at the current rate.

**Is there a minimum purchase?**
On Gate's spot market there's no meaningful minimum — the constraint is the order size that makes the fee worth paying. Card purchases through third-party providers usually have their own small minimums set by the provider.

**Which pair should I use, TIA/USDT or TIA/USDC?**
TIA/USDT is the deeper book on nearly every venue. USDC gives you a slightly different settlement asset but usually less liquidity, and for a simple buy order that difference is what you'll feel.

**Do I have to complete KYC?**
On Gate, yes — identity verification unlocks higher trading limits and more payment channels, and requirements vary by region and method [5]. Buying through a wallet or DEX avoids KYC, but you're trading away the fiat on-ramp and the support desk to get there.

**Can I stake TIA on an exchange?**
Celestia supports staking natively as a Cosmos chain. Whether a specific product is offered depends on the platform and the region — check the earn section of your account rather than assuming.
